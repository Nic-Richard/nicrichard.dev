TraceTray

Behavioural analytics

Live beta

Behavioural analytics and UX insights from a lightweight interaction tracker

TraceTray came from wondering how much a website could learn from simple raw interaction data such as cursor movement, clicks, and scrolling. Instead of stopping at activity counts, it analyzes those signals together to show how visitors pay attention and move through a site.

The goal is to turn a lightweight stream of activity into behavioural analytics that can help explain hesitation, engagement, and possible UX problems.

Built around

A lightweight tracker and analysis pipeline that turns raw interaction signals into behavioural findings and UX insight.

Stack

Node.js, Express, MongoDB, Python, scikit-learn, pandas

WikiRacr

Graph search and multiplayer game

Live

A competitive Wikipedia game built from a graph-search project

WikiRacr started with researching a fast way to find the shortest path between two Wikipedia articles. I built forward and reverse graphs and used bidirectional breadth-first search across the Wikipedia link graph, which made it practical to generate and curate playable pairs at the scale of millions of articles.

That work became the foundation for a full game where players navigate by clicking links only. I’m especially proud of the flow of the site and how the pair system supports solo play, daily challenges, and multiplayer modes.

Built around

A curated pair pipeline backed by bidirectional search over forward and reverse Wikipedia link graphs.

Stack

React, Vite, Node.js, Socket.IO, SQLite, Clerk, Stripe

Portlore

Geographic data and travel planning

Live, data in progress

A cruise port guide built around finding what is actually useful near where passengers disembark

Portlore started as a guide for cruise passengers visiting a city for only part of a day. Building it meant starting with ports around the world, resolving practical passenger terminals, and gathering large sets of nearby places before refining them into useful destination guides.

The site presents the final selection through a visual map and itinerary interface, helping passengers decide what is worth visiting and what they can realistically fit into their time ashore.

Built around

A port-to-guide data pipeline that resolves where passengers arrive and filters nearby places into useful recommendations.

Stack

JavaScript, Node.js, Express, OpenStreetMap, Google Places and Routes, Anthropic
